# Adaptive style selection

Use this reference to choose a visual direction. Treat the guidance as a starting point, not a template.

## Selection sequence

1. Identify the primary user and the consequence of mistakes.
2. Identify the dominant task: explore, decide, transact, monitor, configure, communicate, or create.
3. Choose the required level of trust, energy, warmth, and information density.
4. Respect any established brand assets and interface patterns.
5. Select visual cues that reinforce those qualities.
6. Remove effects that do not support comprehension or brand character.

## Product and industry starting points

| Context | Prioritize | Useful visual cues | Common failure to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| B2B SaaS, CRM, operations | Speed, clarity, data density, status visibility | Restrained palette, crisp type, compact controls, strong tables, clear semantic states | Oversized marketing layout inside the product |
| Finance, insurance, lending | Trust, precision, transparency, error prevention | Stable composition, conservative accent, tabular numerals, explicit totals and confirmations | Hiding important terms or using playful ambiguity |
| Healthcare and care services | Calm, reassurance, privacy, legibility | Soft but clear contrast, humane imagery, plain language, generous form spacing | Making the interface sterile, faint, or childish |
| Legal, government, compliance | Authority, traceability, accessibility | Structured typography, obvious steps, timestamps, document-first patterns | Decorative effects that weaken credibility |
| Ecommerce and marketplaces | Product discovery, comparison, conversion | Product-first imagery, clear price hierarchy, visible delivery/trust details, decisive CTAs | Promotions overpowering product understanding |
| Local and professional services | Trust, proximity, rapid contact | Authentic proof, service area, direct contact/booking, clear credentials | Generic stock imagery and vague claims |
| Education | Progress, comprehension, encouragement | Clear learning paths, visible progress, calm reading surfaces, supportive feedback | Gamification that distracts from learning |
| Hospitality, travel, real estate | Desire, atmosphere, confident decisions | High-quality imagery, editorial rhythm, concise facts, map/location context | Image-heavy pages that obscure practical details |
| Creative, media, fashion | Identity, emotion, memorable browsing | Bold art direction, expressive type, asymmetry with disciplined rhythm | Novelty that damages navigation and readability |
| Developer tools and technical products | Efficiency, credibility, scannability | Monospace accents, precise hierarchy, keyboard affordances, code/data contrast | “Hacker” styling that reduces legibility |
| Consumer mobile | Immediacy, familiarity, touch comfort | Focused screens, strong bottom-reach actions, friendly feedback, progressive disclosure | Desktop layouts squeezed into a phone |

## Style axes

Choose intentionally rather than defaulting to the middle of every axis.

- **Restrained ↔ expressive:** Base this on brand differentiation and emotional intent.
- **Dense ↔ spacious:** Base this on task frequency and data volume, not a universal preference for whitespace.
- **Institutional ↔ approachable:** Base this on authority needs and the user’s anxiety level.
- **Editorial ↔ product-led:** Use editorial composition for narrative and discovery; use product-led composition for repeated actions.
- **Angular ↔ soft:** Use geometry to support brand character; keep control states unmistakable.
- **Monochrome ↔ color-forward:** Use color as structure and meaning before decoration.

## Typography selection

- Choose readable, well-supported fonts that fit the brand character and language coverage.
- Pair fonts only when the roles are clear; otherwise use one strong family with weight and size contrast.
- Use tighter display leading and comfortable body leading.
- Use tabular numerals for changing metrics, prices, and aligned financial data.
- Avoid fashionable typefaces when they damage long-form or small-size readability.

## Color selection

- Start with neutral canvas and surface roles, then add one primary accent and semantic colors.
- Increase color intensity near actions and important states; keep large reading surfaces calmer.
- Ensure dark mode is designed with its own elevation and contrast logic rather than mechanically inverting colors.
- Validate state colors for contrast and redundancy through icon, text, or shape.

## Originality without novelty tax

Create identity through a small number of repeatable choices: typography, grid, image treatment, border language, icon weight, motion character, or a signature composition. Keep navigation and task controls familiar unless a different pattern measurably improves use.
